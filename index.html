Covariance: Σ[(xᵢ − x̄)(yᵢ − ȳ)] / (n − 1)
Pearson: r = Cov(X,Y) / (sₓ × sᵧ)

When both axes are standardized using sample standard deviations, their sample covariance equals Pearson's r.

Explore different relationships

Try multiplying X by 10. The covariance becomes 10 times larger, but r stays the same. Standardize the axes to see both variables measured in standard deviations.

Three ideas to remember

1. Direction

Positive covariance: high X tends to accompany high Y. Negative covariance: high X tends to accompany low Y.

2. Measurement scale

Covariance changes if you change units. Correlation is standardized and does not change under positive rescaling.

3. Linear, not necessarily causal

A correlation of zero does not rule out a nonlinear relationship. Neither covariance nor correlation alone establishes causation.
